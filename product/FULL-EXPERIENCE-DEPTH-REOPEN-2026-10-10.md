## Product experience proposal — "The Last Performance"

**Type:** playable physical shadow-puppet theatre, with lighting, paper puppetry, reactive backstage stage elements, miniature cast, an audience and two different endings. NOT a sequence of 3 motion demos.

**Setting:** handcrafted vaudeville/folk paper theatre at night — visible wood stage, worn crimson curtain, vellum scrim, safety pin hinges, lamp, pulley cords, painted title playbill, footlight, audience silhouette row and backstage props. Art identity defined by paper cut edges, wax-painted faces, real cloth/wood material, deliberately asymmetric hand animation. No generic haunted dashboard/fog.

**Role and stakes:** user is the last stage operator: conduct a final puppet performance and close the curtain before midnight. A little paper puppet **Marlow** (working name) wants to take its last bow; the shadow cast behind it gradually insists on rewriting the play. An authored stage playbill provides goal and cues diegetically. The user is emotionally invested in a character and the show outcome, not tapping for a counter.

## Layered interactive show (60–120s main path, optional prop discovery)

1. **House lights / orientation (0–5s):** hand-illustrated stage alive, audience eyes follow, paper tickets shiver, strings are visibly draggable, lamp casts appropriate relationship. Instantly playable.
2. **Act I / perform (5–20s):** pull puppet string, head/arm responds and shadow follows. Performance receives reaction from audience, puppet expression changes, playbill updates physically, optional puppet prop cue to teach agency.
3. **Act II / refusal (20–45s):** on attempted bow, shadow hesitates then holds the arm higher than the performer. Spotlight/cloth stage reacts materially; audience stops clapping and the playbill ink rewrites a cue. Player can inspect/pull a backstage lever to reassert control: that action has a consequence, not decorative hover.
4. **Act III / reversal (45–70s):** shadow takes a cord, puppet's articulated joints are manipulated **without player's control** by the Rive State Machine while player can try to resist; true reversed input/cause is observable. Stage ropes visibly change direction, paper cutouts shift. The "hand" of the shadow manipulates the physically connected puppet, not a rendered ghost overlay.
5. **Operator decision (70–95s):** **CUT STRINGS** gives the puppet freedom while shadow remains trapped behind scrim; **EXTINGUISH LAMP** releases autonomous shadow across the stage while puppet freezes, an uncanny different ending. Both should have distinct authored character/environment reactions and permanent stage state until replay. No premature guaranteed happy ending.
6. **Curtain call / replay:** curtain physically falls, in-world playbill stamps a unique ending, audience exits/responds. Replay resets all limbs, relationships and light, and allows exploring the alternate ending.
